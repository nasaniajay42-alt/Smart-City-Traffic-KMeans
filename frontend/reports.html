Mathematical Foundation of K-Means Clustering
Academic Theory
1. Objective Function & Inertia Optimization

K-Means aims to partition \(n\) observations into \(k\) clusters \(S = \{S_1, S_2, \dots, S_k\}\) so as to minimize the within-cluster sum of squares (WCSS / Inertia):

\(J = \sum_{j=1}^{k} \sum_{x \in S_j} ||x - \mu_j||^2\)

Where \(\mu_j\) represents the geometric centroid of cluster \(S_j\). The optimization alternates between cluster reassignment (\(x \to \arg\min ||x - \mu_j||^2\)) and centroid recomputation (\(\mu_j = \frac{1}{|S_j|} \sum_{x \in S_j} x\)) until convergence.

2. K-Means++ Smart Initialization

Standard random initialization can converge to suboptimal local minima. This system implements K-Means++ initialization:

  • Choose the first center \(\mu_1\) uniformly at random from dataset \(X\).
  • For each point \(x\), compute the distance \(D(x)\) to the closest already chosen center.
  • Select the next center with probability proportional to \(D(x)^2\):
    \(P(x) = \frac{D(x)^2}{\sum_{x' \in X} D(x')^2}\)
  • Repeat until all \(k\) initial centroids are selected.
3. StandardScaler Feature Normalization

Because Euclidean distance is sensitive to variance scales, features with large numeric ranges dominate the metric:

\(z = \frac{x - \mu}{\sigma}\)

Standardizing Age (\(18–70\)), Income (\(\$15\text{k}–\$137\text{k}\)), and Spending (\(1–100\)) guarantees that every feature dimension carries balanced mathematical weight in geometric distance calculations.

4. Silhouette Coefficient Cohesion Metric

For sample \(i\), let \(a(i)\) be the mean intra-cluster distance and \(b(i)\) be the mean nearest-cluster distance:

\(s(i) = \frac{b(i) - a(i)}{\max(a(i), b(i))}\)

A high score (\(s(i) \to +1\)) proves that customers within a cluster are tightly grouped and distinctly separated from neighboring clusters.
